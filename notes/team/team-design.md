sources:
  - title: "How Do Committees Invent?"
    author: Melvin E. Conway
    url: "https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html"
    type: paper
    year: 1968
    note: "The Datamation paper that states what became Conway's law: organisations produce designs that copy their communication structures. It sets out the mapping between system and organisation, and how overstaffing breaks communication and then the design."
  - title: "Conway's Law"
    author: Martin Fowler
    url: "https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ConwaysLaw.html"
    type: article
    year: 2022
    note: "A short explanation of why the law holds and three ways to respond, including the Inverse Conway Maneuver. It notes that groups of a dozen or two communicate informally, so the law matters most in larger structures."
  - title: "Key Concepts"
    author: Team Topologies
    url: "https://teamtopologies.com/key-concepts"
    type: framework
    year: 2026
    note: "The Team Topologies company's summary of its approach: four team types, three interaction modes, and principles such as respecting cognitive limits and keeping teams stable. It says the approach is not a method for drawing a new org chart. The site also promotes the company's courses and workshops."
  - title: "Spotify's Failed #SquadGoals"
    author: Jeremiah Lee
    url: "https://www.jeremiahlee.com/posts/failed-squad-goals/"
    type: article
    year: 2020
    note: "A former Spotify product manager on why the Spotify model was mostly aspirational and should not be copied. It cites former Spotify coaches and co-authors of the model, and covers matrix management, team autonomy and collaboration."
  - title: "Sizing engineering teams."
    author: Will Larson
    url: "https://lethain.com/sizing-engineering-teams/"
    type: article
    year: 2018
    note: "Rules of thumb for team size, such as six to eight engineers per manager and about eight people for an on-call rotation. It argues that teams under four behave like individuals and break when someone leaves, and gives a playbook for growing and splitting a team."
  - title: "The Power of Proximity to Coworkers"
    author: Natalia Emanuel, Emma Harrington, Amanda Pallais
    url: "https://www.nber.org/papers/w31880"
    type: paper
    year: 2026
    note: "A study of software engineers at a Fortune 500 firm, using office closures and return-to-office mandates as natural experiments. Sitting near teammates raised coding feedback by 18.3% and improved code quality, mostly for younger and less-tenured engineers, while experienced engineers wrote less code."
  - title: "The 37signals Guide to Internal Communication"
    author: 37signals
    url: "https://basecamp.com/guides/how-we-communicate"
    type: guide
    year: 2026
    note: "How a remote company communicates internally: thirty principles favouring asynchronous, long-form writing, then its routines. These include daily and weekly check-ins and six-week team summaries. The toolset is the company's own product, Basecamp."
