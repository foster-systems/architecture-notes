sources:
  - title: "How Do Committees Invent?"
    author: Melvin E. Conway
    url: "https://www.melconway.com/Home/Committees_Paper.html"
    type: paper
    year: 1968
    note: "The Datamation paper that states what became Conway's law: a system's design copies the communication structure of the organisation that builds it. It sets out the mapping between system and organisation, and how overstaffing breaks communication and then the design."
  - title: "Conway's Law"
    author: Martin Fowler
    url: "https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ConwaysLaw.html"
    type: article
    year: 2022
    note: "A short explanation of why the law holds and three ways to respond, including the Inverse Conway Maneuver. It notes that a dozen or two people communicate informally, so the law matters once people need organising into teams."
  - title: "Team Topologies"
    author: Martin Fowler
    url: "https://martinfowler.com/bliki/TeamTopologies.html"
    type: article
    year: 2023
    note: "A summary of Matthew Skelton and Manuel Pais's model of four team types and three interaction modes, reviewed by the two authors. It names the model's main insight: a platform exists to reduce the cognitive load on teams aligned to a business stream. It also says where the model simplifies."
  - title: "Sizing engineering teams."
    author: Will Larson
    url: "https://lethain.com/sizing-engineering-teams/"
    type: article
    year: 2018
    note: "Rules of thumb for team size, such as six to eight engineers per manager and eight people for a 24/7 on-call rotation. It argues that teams under four behave like individuals and break when someone leaves, and gives a playbook for growing and splitting a team."
  - title: "Spotify's Failed #SquadGoals"
    author: Jeremiah Lee
    url: "https://www.jeremiahlee.com/posts/failed-squad-goals/"
    type: article
    year: 2020
    note: "A former Spotify product manager on why the Spotify model was mostly aspirational and should not be copied. It cites former Spotify coaches and a co-author of the model, and covers matrix management, team autonomy and collaboration."
  - title: "Hybrid working from home improves retention without damaging performance"
    author: Nicholas Bloom, Ruobing Han, James Liang
    url: "https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07500-2"
    agents: "https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11208135/fullTextXML"
    type: paper
    year: 2024
    note: "A randomised trial with 1,612 engineering, marketing and finance staff at Trip.com, where half could work from home two days a week. Hybrid work cut quit rates by a third and did not change performance reviews, promotions or lines of code written. James Liang co-founded and chairs Trip.com."
  - title: "The Power of Proximity to Coworkers"
    author: Natalia Emanuel, Emma Harrington, Amanda Pallais
    url: "https://www.nber.org/papers/w31880"
    type: paper
    year: 2026
    note: "A study of software engineers at a Fortune 500 firm, using office closures and return-to-office mandates as natural experiments. Sitting near teammates raised coding feedback by 18.3% and improved code quality, mostly for younger and less-tenured engineers, while experienced engineers wrote less code."
