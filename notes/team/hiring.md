  - title: "Revisiting the design of selection systems in light of new findings regarding the validity of widely used predictors"
    author: Paul R. Sackett, Charlene Zhang, Christopher M. Berry, Filip Lievens
    url: "https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/industrial-and-organizational-psychology/article/revisiting-the-design-of-selection-systems-in-light-of-new-findings-regarding-the-validity-of-widely-used-predictors/A20984B138319E3D432E643978BF026D"
    type: paper
    year: 2023
    note: "A peer-reviewed article on what the authors' 2022 re-analysis of meta-analyses means for designing hiring. In the corrected estimates, structured interviews predict job performance best, and job-specific methods such as job knowledge tests and work samples beat general tests of ability and personality. Most estimates are lower than the figures long quoted from Schmidt and Hunter (1998)."
  - title: "A guide to structured interviewing for better hiring practices"
    author: Google re:Work
    url: "https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/a-guide-to-structured-interviewing-for-better-hiring-practices"
    type: guide
    year: 2026
    note: "Google's guide to structured interviews: the same vetted questions for every candidate, written notes, scoring against rubrics, and trained interviewers. It explains behavioural and hypothetical questions and how to write a rubric. The internal results it reports, such as 40 minutes saved per interview, are Google's own."
  - title: "The Hiring Post"
    author: Thomas Ptacek
    url: "https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2015/03/06/the-hiring-post/"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "Ptacek's argument that developer interviews are unreliable, with Matasano as the example. It covers warming up candidates, standardised work-sample tests scored with a rubric, and scripted interviews. It ends with a checklist for judging any hiring process."
  - title: "Your hiring funnel."
    author: Will Larson
    url: "https://lethain.com/hiring-funnel/"
    type: article
    year: 2018
    note: "A model of hiring as four steps: identify, motivate, evaluate and close. It shows how to measure each step, and extends the funnel past the offer to onboarding, impact, promotion and retention."
  - title: "How and why I interviewed engineers at Workbrew"
    author: Mike McQuaid
    url: "https://mikemcquaid.com/how-and-why-i-interviewed-engineers-at-workbrew/"
    type: article
    year: 2025
    note: "A co-founder's account of hiring the first five engineers at a small remote-first company. Each stage is scored with a numeric rubric, from emailed technical questions to live pairing on the real codebase and an interview with all three founders. AI tools were allowed in the pairing round if disclosed, and every hire came through the founders' networks."
  - title: "Yes, You Can Use AI in Our Interviews. In fact, we insist"
    author: Simon Newton (Canva)
    url: "https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/yes-you-can-use-ai-in-our-interviews/"
    type: article
    year: 2025
    note: "Canva's account of replacing its algorithms screening round with an interview in which candidates are expected to use AI tools. The new questions are larger and more ambiguous, and interviewers assess how candidates clarify requirements and check generated code. The reported results are the company's own."
  - title: "Onboarding and the Cost of Team Debt"
    author: Kate Heddleston
    url: "https://www.kateheddleston.com/blog/onboarding-and-the-cost-of-team-debt"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "Defines team debt, the inefficiency that builds when new people are not trained and integrated. It sets reliable independence as the goal of onboarding and lists practices such as shared mentoring, shadowing and pair programming. Based on a talk at PyCon 2014."
