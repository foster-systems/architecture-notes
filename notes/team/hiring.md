sources:
  - title: "The Hiring Post"
    author: Thomas Ptacek
    url: "https://sockpuppet.org/blog/2015/03/06/the-hiring-post/"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "Ptacek's argument that developer interviews are unreliable, with Matasano as the example. It covers warming up candidates, standardised work-sample tests scored with a rubric, and scripted interviews. It ends with a checklist for judging any hiring process."
  - title: "Our Hiring Process"
    author: Fly.io
    url: "https://docs.fly.io/hiring/hiring"
    type: docs
    year: 2026
    note: "Fly.io's own account of its process: two or three untimed take-home challenges graded against rubrics, then a typed structured interview in Slack. Resumes are not evaluated and salaries are set by level. A short, concrete example of a work-sample process."
  - title: "How and why I interviewed engineers at Workbrew"
    author: Mike McQuaid
    url: "https://mikemcquaid.com/how-and-why-i-interviewed-engineers-at-workbrew/"
    type: article
    year: 2025
    note: "An account of hiring five engineers at a small remote-first company with three founders. It walks through each stage, from the job posting and emailed technical questions to live pairing on the actual codebase and a founders' interview. AI tools were allowed in the pairing round if disclosed."
  - title: "Yes, You Can Use AI in Our Interviews. In fact, we insist"
    author: Simon Newton (Canva)
    url: "https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/yes-you-can-use-ai-in-our-interviews/"
    type: article
    year: 2025
    note: "Canva's account of changing its technical interviews so that candidates are expected to use AI tools. It describes the harder, more ambiguous questions and a new round that assesses how candidates clarify requirements and review generated code. The reported results are the company's own."
  - title: "Your hiring funnel."
    author: Will Larson
    url: "https://lethain.com/hiring-funnel/"
    type: article
    year: 2018
    note: "A model of hiring as four steps: identify, motivate, evaluate and close. It shows how to measure each step, and extends the funnel past the offer to onboarding, impact, promotion and retention."
  - title: "Hiring and the market for lemons"
    author: Dan Luu
    url: "https://danluu.com/hiring-lemons/"
    type: article
    year: 2016
    note: "A reply to the claim that great developers are never on the market, using Akerlof's market for lemons. It names four causes behind the hiring complaints Luu has seen: low pay, filters that reject good candidates, rare skill combinations and unseen dysfunction. The essay says it is written from a developer's side."
  - title: "Onboarding and the Cost of Team Debt"
    author: Kate Heddleston
    url: "https://www.kateheddleston.com/blog/onboarding-and-the-cost-of-team-debt"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "Defines team debt, the inefficiency that builds when new people are not trained and integrated. It sets reliable independence as the goal of onboarding and lists practices such as shared mentoring, shadowing and pair programming. Based on a talk at PyCon 2014."
