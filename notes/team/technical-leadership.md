  - title: "What does a startup CTO actually do?"
    author: Eric Ries
    url: "https://www.startuplessonslearned.com/2008/09/what-does-startup-cto-actually-do.html"
    type: article
    year: 2008
    note: "Ries defines the job as making the technology strategy serve the business strategy. He lists five skills, from platform choice and finding the 80/20 to growing technical leads, and adds a sixth he calls controversial: owning the development methodology. He explains why he took the CTO title expecting not to manage people, then learned to manage."
  - title: "On the role of CTO"
    author: Camille Fournier
    url: "https://www.elidedbranches.com/2015/02/cto.html"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "The author of The Manager's Path defines the role as the strategic technical executive the company needs at its current stage. She argues that the CTO is not the top of the engineering ladder and need not be the best engineer. She warns that a CTO who hands all management to VPs loses the power to put people on what matters."
  - title: "The CTO Journey at a Small Startup"
    author: Bryan Helmig
    url: "https://zapier.com/blog/startup-cto/"
    type: article
    year: 2017
    note: "Zapier's co-founder and CTO maps the role by team size: 1 to 2 engineers, 2 to 6, 6 to 12, then 12 and up. A table shows the share of time spent coding at each stage. Past 12 engineers he describes a fork between a VP of Engineering path and a CTO path, and how to choose."
  - title: "#define CTO"
    author: Greg Brockman
    url: "https://blog.gregbrockman.com/figuring-out-the-cto-role-at-stripe"
    type: article
    year: 2014
    note: "Brockman's account of going from early engineer to CTO at Stripe and hiring a VP of Engineering at about 25 engineers to take over one-on-ones and recruiting. It covers delegating, losing visibility into the organisation, and returning to code. It reports what about 20 other CTOs and VPs of Engineering said about the role."
  - title: "Differences between VP Engineering and CTO"

He gives the team sizes at which he thinks each layer becomes necessary. He sets out how Malt split the work and the alternatives to the hire, such as a principal engineer."
