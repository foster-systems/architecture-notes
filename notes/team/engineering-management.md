  - title: "Following the data: The research behind great managers"
    author: "Sarah Calderon, Debbie Newhouse (Google)"
    url: "https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/following-the-data-the-research-behind-great-managers"
    type: article
    year: 2025
    note: "Google's summary of two decades of its internal research on managers. Project Oxygen analysed over 10,000 data points from performance reviews and feedback surveys, plus interview notes, found that teams with highly effective managers got better results, were happier and quit less, and listed ten behaviours of such managers, such as coaching and not micromanaging. It also covers Project Aristotle on team effectiveness and the three manager responsibilities Google built from the research. The findings are Google's own."
  - title: "The Engineer/Manager Pendulum"
    author: Charity Majors
    url: "https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/"
    type: article
    year: 2017
    note: "Honeycomb's co-founder argues that management is a change of profession, not a promotion, and that engineers can move between management and hands-on work more than once. She holds that the best frontline managers are never more than two or three years from hands-on work. She calls the hybrid manager and tech lead unstable, because engineering skills decay while managing and the two kinds of work cannot be done at once."
  - title: "The Update, The Vent, and The Disaster"
    author: Michael Lopp
    url: "https://randsinrepose.com/archives/the-update-the-vent-and-the-disaster/"
    type: article
    year: 2010
    note: "Lopp's guide to weekly one-on-ones: same time each week, always held, at least 30 minutes. He sorts them into three kinds by the report's mood, an update, a vent and a disaster, and says how a manager should respond to each. He argues that a one-on-one is not a status report but a place to catch dissatisfaction early."
  - title: "Feedback Equation"
    author: Lara Hogan
    url: "https://larahogan.me/blog/feedback-equation/"
    type: article
    year: 2018
    note: "A three-part structure for feedback: an observation of behaviour stated as fact, its impact on what the recipient cares about, and a question or request. It is close to the Situation-Behaviour-Impact model and comes with a worksheet. Hogan sells feedback workshops and courses."
  - title: "Underperformance"
    author: GitLab
    url: "https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/leadership/underperformance/"
    type: docs
    year: 2025
    note: "GitLab's public handbook page on managing underperformance. It lists warning signs, separates skill-based from will-based causes, and expects managers to act early with coaching, involve the people team and their own manager, and follow local labour law. It says that after documented coaching GitLab often moves to separation without a formal performance improvement plan."
  - title: "The Right Way to Lay People Off"
    author: Ben Horowitz
    url: "https://a16z.com/the-right-way-to-lay-people-off/"
    type: article
    year: 2010
    note: "Horowitz on the three layoffs at Loudcloud and Opsware, which cost 400 jobs, and how the company kept its culture through them. His steps include acting fast once decided, naming the company's failure rather than individual performance as the cause, having each manager lay off their own people, and the CEO addressing the whole company first. Horowitz co-founded the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz, which publishes the post."
