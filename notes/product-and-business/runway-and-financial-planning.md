why: "A startup dies when it runs out of cash, and founders often find out too late to raise more or cut costs. Hiring is usually the largest cost, so each hiring plan is also a decision about runway."

sources:
  - title: "Default Alive or Default Dead?"
    author: Paul Graham
    url: "https://paulgraham.com/aord.html"
    type: article
    year: 2015
    note: "An essay asking whether a startup, with constant expenses and its recent revenue growth, reaches profitability on the money it has left. It argues that founders should ask this early, keep a written plan B in case fundraising fails, and that hiring too fast is the biggest killer of startups that raise money."
  - title: "Burn Rate"
    author: Fred Wilson
    url: "https://avc.com/2011/12/burn-rate/"
    type: article
    year: 2011
    note: "A venture investor's MBA Mondays post defining burn rate and months of cash left, with worked numbers. It contrasts a quick estimate from two cash balances with gross and net burn built from the income statement and cash outlays, and says to check one against the other. It treats one-time expenses that recur as regular burn."
  - title: "Kirsty Nathoo - Managing Startup Finances"
    author: Kirsty Nathoo, Y Combinator
    url: "https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LBC16jhiwak"
    agents: "https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/startup-school-week-5-recap-kirsty-nathoo-and-kevin-hale"
    type: talk
    year: 2019
    note: "A Startup School lecture by Kirsty Nathoo, then Y Combinator's CFO, on six common money mistakes she sees in startups. They range from not tracking bank balance, burn, runway and growth every week to understating expenses, handing finances to someone else, scaling too fast and fundraising too late. YC's Week 5 podcast recap has a transcript."
  - title: "SaaS Financial Plan 2.0"
    author: Christoph Janz
    url: "https://christophjanz.blogspot.com/2016/03/saas-financial-plan-20.html"
    type: tool
    year: 2016
    note: "A free spreadsheet template for an early-stage SaaS financial plan, based on a model the author built for Zendesk, with a post explaining its tabs and assumptions. It plans revenue by pricing tier, headcount, monthly recurring revenue movements and cash flow from a starting bank balance and financings. The author is an investor at the venture firm Point Nine."
  - title: "The Burn Multiple"
    author: David Sacks
    url: "https://sacks.substack.com/p/the-burn-multiple-51a7e43cb200"
    type: article
    year: 2020
    note: "Proposes the burn multiple, net burn divided by net new ARR, as a measure of how much a startup burns for each dollar of growth. It gives rule-of-thumb ranges, shows how gross margin, sales efficiency or churn problems raise the multiple, and expects it to fall as the company matures. The author founded Yammer and was PayPal's founding COO."
  - title: "Advice for Companies With Less Than 1 Year of Runway"
    author: Dalton Caldwell
    url: "https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/advice-startups-running-out-of-money/"
    type: article
    year: 2016
    note: "Advice from Dalton Caldwell, then a Y Combinator partner, for funded startups with 12 months of runway or less. It lists beliefs that delay cost cuts, says payroll is the usual source of high burn, and explains why waiting weakens the company's position with investors and acquirers. Below about two months of cash it advises an orderly shutdown that pays employees, taxes and debts."
