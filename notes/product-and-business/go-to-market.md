title: Go-to-market and early sales
description: "How a software company finds its first customers, matches its way of selling to its price point, and moves from founder-led sales to a sales team."
why: "Price per customer decides how a product must be sold, from self-service to field sales, and that choice shapes the product. Hiring salespeople before the company knows how customers buy burns cash without adding revenue."

sources:
  - title: "Do Things that Don't Scale"
    author: Paul Graham
    url: "https://paulgraham.com/ds.html"
    type: article
    year: 2013
    note: "An essay arguing that startups take off because their founders make them take off, first by recruiting users by hand. It draws on Y Combinator companies, such as Stripe's founders setting up new users on the spot and Airbnb's going door to door. It also covers giving early users unusual attention and manual work done before it is automated."
  - title: "How today's fastest growing B2B businesses found their first ten customers"
    author: Lenny Rachitsky
    url: "https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses"
    type: article
    year: 2020
    note: "Quotes from founders and early employees of more than 20 B2B companies, among them Figma, Stripe, Airtable, Shopify and Slack, on how they found their first ten customers, some taken from earlier interviews. The author sorts them into three levers: the founders' own network, going where customers already gather, and press. It is a free edition of a paid newsletter."
  - title: "Five ways to build a $100 million business"
    author: Christoph Janz
    url: "https://christophjanz.blogspot.com/2014/10/five-ways-to-build-100-million-business.html"
    type: article
    year: 2014
    note: "Shows five combinations of customer count and annual revenue per customer that reach $100 million a year, from 1,000 enterprises paying $100,000 to 10 million users monetized through ads. It explains what acquiring each kind of customer takes: virality, trial sign-ups, inside sales or enterprise field sales. The author co-founded the venture firm Point Nine."
  - title: "Startups Need a Special Learning Curve for Sales"
    author: Marguerite Rigoglioso, Stanford Graduate School of Business
    url: "https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/startups-need-special-learning-curve-sales"
    type: article
    year: 2006
    note: "Stanford's summary of the sales learning curve, from Mark Leslie and Charles Holloway's 2006 Harvard Business Review article. It argues that a new company must first learn how customers buy and use its product, so it should start with a few salespeople comfortable with ambiguity and hire faster only once sales yields rise. Leslie founded Veritas Software."
  - title: "Founding Sales: The Founder Led Sales & Early Stage Go-to-Market Handbook"
    author: Pete Kazanjy
    url: "https://www.foundingsales.com/table-of-contents"
    type: guide
    year: 2020
    note: "A free online book on B2B sales for founders, by a founder who sold TalentBin's product himself, then built its sales team and led sales after Monster Worldwide bought it. It runs from prospecting, pitching and closing to customer success, early sales management, sales hiring and onboarding. It warns against hiring sales staff before the founder has proven the sales motion over a few dozen deals. The author also sells paid editions of the book, video classes and a chatbot on founder-led sales."
