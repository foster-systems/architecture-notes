    author: Paul Graham

    type: article

    note: "An essay on raising a company's first substantial round, after initial seed money, written as the advice Y Combinator gives its startups before Demo Day.   - title: "A Guide to Seed Fundraising"

    url: "https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/"

    year: 2016
    note: "A summary of what founders need to know to raise seed money, by a former President of Y Combinator. It covers when to raise, how much, convertible notes, SAFEs and priced rounds, valuation, meeting investors and closing. Its round sizes, valuations and costs, such as about $15k a month for an engineer, are 2016 figures."
  - title: "The SAFE: The standard way startups raise money"
    author: Y Combinator
    url: "https://www.ycombinator.com/documents"
    type: docs
    year: 2026
    note: "Y Combinator's free standard forms for the post-money SAFE, an agreement that turns an investment now into shares at a later priced round. It has US versions with a valuation cap, a discount or an MFN term, a pro rata side letter, and a user guide with sample conversion math. Versions for Canada, the Cayman Islands and Singapore are listed, with advice to consult a local lawyer."
  - title: "A Standard and Clean Series A Term Sheet"
    author: Jason Kwon
    url: "https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/a-standard-and-clean-series-a-term-sheet"
    type: article
    year: 2019
    note: "A sample Series A term sheet with standard terms, by the general counsel of YC Continuity, Y Combinator's growth fund, with a Word version. It explains what control terms, such as board composition, and non-standard economic terms, such as liquidation preferences above 1x, signal about an investor."
  - title: "Model Legal Documents"
    author: National Venture Capital Association
    url: "https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/"
    type: docs
    year: 2026
    note: "Free model documents for a priced US venture round: certificate of incorporation, stock purchase agreement, investors' rights, voting, and right of first refusal and co-sale agreements. The association of US venture investors describes them as a starting point to be tailored, and each lists its last update."
  - title: "How To Do Startup Technical Due Diligence"

    type: article
    year: 2017

It covers the questions investors want answered, interviews with the CEO and technology leaders, and how to assess code and operations. It ends with the structure he uses for the final report."
  - title: "A prep checklist for startups about to undergo technical due diligence"

    note: "A checklist of the questions and documents investors ask for in technical due diligence, written for the company being reviewed. It covers the roadmap, code quality, intellectual property, security, the development process and infrastructure. The author runs Sema, which sells codebase analysis for deals; TechCrunch published it as a guest column."
